PART 1.5 — THE 6-YEAR-OLD TEST (the bar drops two years)
============================================================

Pass 4 set the 8-year-old bar. The owner has lowered it: **a 6-year-old with
no musical experience figures it out, frictionlessly.** What changes at six:

- An 8-year-old reads labels; a 6-year-old reads PICTURES AND MOTION first.
  Every primary act must be legible with the words covered: the big gold
  circle plays, the lit line is where we are, the sliders make voices louder.
- An 8-year-old explores; a 6-year-old follows THE LIT PATH. At every moment
  exactly one thing on screen visibly invites the next tap. Two invitations
  is a fork; a fork is a failure.
- An 8-year-old recovers from a wrong tap; a 6-year-old STOPS. So a wrong
  tap must be impossible to fear: nothing reachable is destructive,
  everything animates back, the words never blame.
- A 6-year-old cannot parse "run-through," "loop mode," "gap," "takes,"
  "enrichment." The practice room speaks five words: **play, sing, part,
  louder, quieter.** (The deep rehearsal tools keep their room — one tap
  deeper, for the musician who goes looking.)

The four contracts (Guess · Result · Words · Courage) still govern. The test
in practice: hand the phone to a 6-year-old with ONE sentence — "this is
your song, want to hear it?" — and watch for the first hesitation. The
hesitation is the finding.

PART 2 — THE PSYCHOLOGY (why the funnel works — the research, applied)
============================================================

The hallway is not a metaphor; it is applied behavioral science. Each
principle below names the mechanic it demands. A surface that violates the
mechanic is fighting human wiring, and will lose.

### 2.1 · Fogg Behavior Model — B = MAP
Behavior happens when Motivation, Ability, and Prompt converge in a moment.
The hallway's whole job is to place the PROMPT at the instant of peak
MOTIVATION and make ABILITY infinite:
- Just filed a hum (motivation peak: "did it work?") → the card pulses: prompt.
- Just heard the base (peak: "I can hear the harmony in my head") →
  "Sing over this" is standing there: prompt.
- Just heard the whole song (peak: pride) → "someone should hear this": prompt.
- **NEW — just added the third part (peak: "what does it all sound like?!")
  → PRACTICE is standing there: prompt.** This was the missing prompt at the
  strongest motivation spike in the product.
Ability for a 6-year-old means: one tap, huge targets, instant sound, no
reading required. Every decision removed raises ability for everyone.

### 2.2 · The Goal-Gradient Effect (Hull, 1932)
Effort accelerates as the goal visibly approaches. The product's goal is A
WHOLE SONG YOU CAN SING. So the goal must be VISIBLE and APPROACHING:
the Final set list growing, the progress ribbon filling section by section
during practice. A user who can see the song assembling works harder to
finish it. A user who can't see it stalls.

### 2.3 · The Zeigarnik Effect — open loops pull
Unfinished sequences occupy the mind. A song with recorded parts that has
NEVER been heard whole is an open loop — it nags, and the Practice button is
the loop-closer. Then, deliberately, finishing practice OPENS the next loop:
"Marcus hasn't heard this." The flywheel is a chain of loops, each closing
one and opening the next: record → hear → layer → hear it whole → share →
their layer arrives → hear it whole again.

### 2.4 · The IKEA Effect (Norton, Mochon, Ariely, 2012)
People overvalue what they helped assemble. Hearing YOUR hum wearing THEIR
harmony inside a WHOLE song is the single highest-value moment this product
can manufacture — the moment love for the product is created. **This is why
practice playing all the parts is not a feature but the point.** A practice
mode that silently drops the harmonies (which is what ships today) destroys
the IKEA payoff at the exact moment it should peak: the writer hears LESS
than what the room showed them they had.

### 2.5 · Hick's Law — choice count is decision time
Decision time grows with the number and complexity of choices. The
6-year-old budget: at most ONE choice per screen, and it should be the
choice they came to make. GarageBand survives its density by showing one
dominant surface per mode. The practice room shows: the words, the play
circle, and nothing else until asked.

### 2.6 · Recognition over recall (Nielsen) — the karaoke law
Nobody should have to remember their own lyrics to sing them. Lyrics on
screen, current line lit, next line waiting — recall becomes recognition,
and a nervous first-time collaborator becomes a participant. Karaoke is not
decoration; it converts listeners into singers, and singers into people who
come back.

### 2.7 · Direct manipulation (Shneiderman) — naive physics
Children (and adults mid-melody) trust interfaces that behave like objects:
the slider moves and the voice gets quieter NOW (30ms ramp, never a click);
the card flies where it was sent; drag follows the finger. Any control whose
effect is delayed, hidden, or textual breaks the physics and the trust.

### 2.8 · Flow state (Csikszentmihalyi) — and the Courage Contract
Flow needs a clear goal, immediate feedback, and NO FEAR. Fear is removed
structurally: nothing destructive in reach, undo in every toast, mistakes
met with "your idea is still here." Fearlessness is manufactured by the UI.

### 2.9 · The Peak-End Rule (Kahneman)
A session is remembered by its emotional peak and its ending. The peak IS
the practice run-through (engineer it to be flawless — gapless, every voice,
in time). The END must land warm and open the next loop: "That's your song."
→ play again · make it better · someone should hear this. Never a dead stop,
never a settings tray as the last thing seen.

### 2.10 · The funnel pattern (Duolingo / Simply Piano / Church Center)
The apps a 6-year-old CAN run never present menus — they present THE NEXT
THING, and the user accepts or ignores it. The hallway already encodes this
as Law 4 ("the next act is standing there"). The practice room extends the
chain to its destination: the room is not a place you operate; it is a path
you are carried down, with the song as the current.

PART 4 — THE PRACTICE ROOM, SCREEN BY SCREEN
============================================================

### 4.1 · THE DOOR (entry — one button, everywhere the song is)
ONE JOB: from anywhere in the song's world, one act starts the whole song.
- The canvas dock's "Practice" and the lift pill both arrive HERE — the
  singing room — not in a settings tray, not in silent Flow. The pill's
  promise ("lift to play") finally tells the truth: lifting PLAYS.
- Final page's finish moment offers "Practice it" alongside "someone should
  hear this."
- A song with nothing recorded lands on the honest, warm "Record a take"
  screen (exists). The door never lies; it just leads.

### 4.2 · THE ROOM AT REST (arrived, not yet playing)
ONE JOB: the song's name, the words, and one giant lit invitation to play.
- Serif song title. Beneath it: the first section's lyrics, big (the
  KaraokeLyrics register — Playfair, clamp'd for a stand or a lap).
- ONE gold: the play circle, bottom-center, 64px+, thumb-arc.
- Quiet, right of transport: "Parts" (the mixer door, ghost). Top: the
  set-down grabber (exists). NOTHING ELSE. No mode picker, no section list,
  no settings. (The deep rehearsal room — loops, speed, takes, metronome —
  remains one quiet tap away: "Practice a part." Hicks: one choice.)

### 4.3 · PLAYING (the karaoke flow)
ONE JOB: the song, whole, every voice, words lighting as they arrive.
- Sections play IN ORDER, GAPLESSLY — one shared Web Audio clock schedules
  each section's base AND all its layers (per-layer alignment offsets
  respected) as one continuous timeline. 500ms of silence between sections
  is an engine defect, not a pacing choice.
- Words: the current section's transcript lines, current line lit (existing
  useSyncedLine), next line dimmed-waiting. No transcript → the section's
  lyrics text, calm. Nothing at all → the section label, large, with the
  maker's name ("Marcus's bridge") — the song is still legible.
- A whisper-thin progress ribbon (the Flow register) with section ticks.
  Tap a tick = the song jumps there (every part re-seats — shared clock).
- Chrome recedes ~2.6s after play (the Flow pattern); any tap returns it.
- The section boundary is FELT, not announced: the next section's label
  breathes in as its first line arrives.

### 4.4 · THE MIXER ("Parts" — the GarageBand room, made human)
ONE JOB: every voice in the song, one row each, volume under the thumb.
- Bottom sheet (focus-trapped, safe-area, three dismissals — the standard).
- One row per sounding memo, GROUPED BY SECTION in play order:
  section label (quiet) → base row → its layer rows, indented one breath.
- A row = the part's name (memo title, or "{maker}'s layer") · the maker's
  color-dot + name · a slider (0–150%, the stack register) · mute (the ear).
  NO dB, NO solo-vs-mute lecture, NO waveforms.
- Sound changes LIVE under the finger (30ms ramped gain — the stack
  player's register). Changes persist per song, per device, instantly —
  the second practice opens exactly as you mixed it.
- The mixer is also the credits page you can touch: every name, every color.

### 4.5 · THE END (peak-end, engineered)
ONE JOB: land the pride, open the next loop.
- The last line settles; a beat of quiet; then, calm and centered:
  "That's your song." — with: Play it again · Make it better (→ the room)
  · Someone should hear this (→ the door/invite).
- Never: a stats card first, a settings tray, an abrupt stop to silence.

### 4.6 · WHAT THE PRACTICE ROOM MUST NEVER SHOW
A BPM without being asked · the word "take," "run-through," "loop," "gap,"
"stack," or "layer-of" · a red anything · a form · two golds · a spinner
where a skeleton or the words could stand · a dead end.

PART 5 — THE ENGINE (ground truth → the assembly, exactly)
============================================================

Traced 2026-08-19 on clean main. Four engines exist; none is this room —
but three contain its organs. The assembly:

1. **The data seam (the one true blocker):** `practiceApi.loadPracticeBundle`
   selects memos WITHOUT `parent_memo_id` — so a base + 3 harmonies arrive
   in practice as 4 swipeable "takes," and every practice surface is
   structurally deaf to layers. FIX: select `parent_memo_id, layer_gain,
   layer_muted, layer_offset_ms`, run `groupIntoStacks()` (exists, pure,
   tested — src/lib/voice/stackModel.ts), and hand each section
   `{ base, layers[] }`. Takes become BASES-only (alternates), each take
   carrying its family. Orphan layers promote (the model already does).
2. **The clock:** generalize the `useStackPlayer` architecture (ONE
   AudioContext, decode → AudioBufferSource per voice, one shared
   `ctx.currentTime` start, per-voice GainNode, 30ms ramps, the
   all-or-nothing element fallback rung, the iOS ≤4-context discipline)
   from "one stack" to "a section-ordered list of stacks on one timeline."
   Section N's voices start at the cumulative offset of sections 0..N-1
   (base durations); layers add their stored alignment offset. Schedule
   current + next; top up as the boundary approaches. Gapless by
   construction.
3. **The words:** `KaraokeLyrics` + `useSyncedLine` already sync a lit line
   to a real position within one section. The room derives
   (songPositionMs → section, sectionPositionMs) from the shared clock and
   feeds the existing component. Flow's tier discipline applies: timestamps
   → lit lines; no timestamps → calm full text; nothing → the label.
4. **The mix:** `resolveMix` (pure, tested) already turns gains+mute+solo
   into per-id targets. The mixer UI lifts from `MemoStack`'s rows. Mix
   persists per song+memo (device-local first — the listen-path precedent).
5. **The cache covenant:** every voice pre-caches via `audioCache`
   (cache-first, `resp.ok`-guarded — the poisoning fix holds). Offline with
   a warm cache: the room plays. Offline cold: honest words, never a spinner.

What this pass does NOT touch: `usePracticePlayer`'s rehearsal machinery
(loops, A/B, speed trainer, takes, mastery, metronome) — that is the deep
room, one tap away, unchanged. Flow stays (the silent music stand has its
audience) — reachable from the deep room as today. DriveMode unchanged.

PART 6 — THE STRESS PROTOCOL (the practice room's Tuesdays)
============================================================

Run as Jordan, one thumb, 390px, every pass:
1. Three-section song, layers on two of them → Practice → count: ONE tap to
   sound; every voice audible; words lighting; zero decisions.
2. Mix it: drag Sarah's harmony down mid-chorus — the change is heard within
   a breath, no click, and survives leaving + reopening the room.
3. Tap the ribbon's last tick — every part re-seats in time (no voice
   drifting, no double-start).
4. BREAK IT: airplane mode with a warm cache (plays) and a cold cache
   (honest words, a path back, never a spinner) · phone call mid-chorus
   (pauses; resume is one tap; position kept) · backgrounded tab (same) ·
   a section whose audio is missing (the words/label still show, the song
   continues — a hole in the audio is never a wall) · double-tap play (one
   song, once) · a 15-section song on a $150 Android (schedule-ahead, not
   decode-everything; no memory cliff) · deny Web Audio (the fallback rung
   plays bases at least — honestly narrated) · reduced motion (no lit-line
   animation; instant jumps) · VoiceOver (the play circle, the ribbon, every
   mixer row speak like humans).
5. The bar at every break: the song keeps its promise or says so in human
   words, the retry is standing there, and recovery returns to the exact
   position. Fix the FIRST hesitation before auditing the next.
